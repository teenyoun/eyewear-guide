Prescription glasses are one of the most price-opaque purchases in America. The same pair can cost $600 at one store and $40 online. Here is exactly what you're paying for, layer by layer, and where the markup hides.

3. Coatings (The Hidden Profit Center)

Anti-reflective coating costs the lab under $5 per lens. Stores charge $50-120 for 'premium' AR. Online: $5-15. Blue light filter: costs $1-3 per lens, stores charge $50-100, online $15-30. This is the biggest single markup in the industry.

Where the $600 Pair's Money Goes

  1. Rent for prime retail space: $100-200
  2. Optician/lab staff salaries: $50-100
  3. Marketing & brand overhead: $50-100
  4. Frame wholesale price: $30-80
  5. Lens wholesale price: $20-50
  6. Actual manufacturing cost: $10-30
  7. Profit: $100-200

How to Pay the Least (While Keeping Quality)

  1. Get your PD measured free — needed for online ordering
  2. Order online: complete quality pairs for $30-80 (see our store comparison)
  3. Buy coatings judiciously: AR coating yes ($5-15 online), blue light only if you're a night screen user
  4. Skip 'premium' lens packages — they're mostly rebranded standard products
  5. Keep the old frame: many online labs re-lens your existing frames for $30-60
The bottom line: You can get functionally identical glasses to a $300+ retail pair for $40-80 online. The difference is service and measurement — valuable for complex prescriptions, irrelevant for standard ones. Read our full $6 vs $400 test for proof.

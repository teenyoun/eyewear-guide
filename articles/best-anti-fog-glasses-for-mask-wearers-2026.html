If the pandemic taught us anything about glasses, it's that mask + glasses = fog. Even post-pandemic, many people continue wearing masks in healthcare settings, during flu season, or while traveling. Here are the most effective anti-fog solutions we tested.

How Anti-Fog Treatments Work

Fog forms when warm, moist air from your breath hits cold lens surfaces, causing water vapor to condense into tiny droplets that scatter light. Anti-fog treatments work by creating a hydrophilic (water-attracting) surface that spreads condensation into a thin, transparent film instead of visible droplets.

Our Testing Method

We tested each product by wearing glasses with a standard surgical mask for 30 minutes in a 68°F room while breathing normally, then repeated the test after walking up three flights of stairs (increased respiration = more fog). Each product was tested fresh and after 4 hours of wear.

What to Avoid

  • Household hacks (shaving cream, soap, toothpaste): These can damage anti-reflective and other lens coatings. The short-term fog reduction isn't worth the long-term lens damage.
  • Spit or saliva: Scuba divers use it, but it's unhygienic and doesn't last on glasses.
  • Glass cleaners with ammonia: Will strip lens coatings over time.

Best Glasses for Square Faces — Frame Shapes That Soften Strong Jawlines

August 06, 2026 · 5 min read

Square face shapes are defined by a strong jawline, broad forehead, and angular features. The goal with glasses is to soften and balance — adding curves where your face has straight lines, and avoiding frames that echo your already-strong angles.

The Golden Rules for Square Faces

  • Do: Round, oval, browline, aviator frames — curves offset angular jawlines and foreheads
  • Don't: Square, rectangular, or geometric frames — they double down on angular features
  • Width: Frames should match or slightly exceed face width at the temples
  • Bridge: A keyhole or saddle bridge softens the nose area; a continuous straight bridge adds harshness

Top Frame Shapes for Square Faces

1. Round Frames

The most flattering choice. Perfectly round lenses create dramatic contrast against square facial features, making the overall appearance more balanced and approachable. Both thin metal rounds (John Lennon style) and thicker acetate rounds work — the former for a subtle intellectual look, the latter for a bold statement.

2. Oval Frames

Similar to round but elongated vertically — adds the illusion of length to a square face. Ideal for people who find pure round frames too retro or extreme. Oval wire frames in gold or silver are particularly flattering on square-faced men.

3. Browline / Clubmaster Frames

The distinctive browline design (thick upper rim, thin or rimless lower) draws attention upward to the brow area, away from the jawline. This is the classic intellectual look — think Malcolm X or Bruce Willis. Works especially well for square-faced men over 40.

4. Aviator Frames

The teardrop shape of aviators has just enough curve to soften a square jaw while remaining masculine and classic. Look for aviators with thin metal frames — thick plastic aviators add bulk that square faces don't need.

Frames to Avoid

  • Square / rectangular / Wayfarer: Echoes your face shape — creates a box-on-box effect
  • Geometric (hexagonal, octagonal): Too many competing angles
  • Very small frames: Make a broad face look proportionally wider

How to Clean Glasses Properly — Avoid Scratches and Damage

August 3, 2026 · 5 min read

You're probably cleaning your glasses wrong. The #1 cause of scratched lenses isn't dropping them — it's improper cleaning. A 2024 survey by the Vision Council found that 73% of glasses wearers use their shirt, paper towel, or dry tissue to clean their lenses, all of which cause micro-scratches that accumulate over time.

The Right Way to Clean Glasses (Step by Step)

Step 1: Wash Your Hands

Oil, dirt, and lotion on your fingers transfer to the lenses. Use lotion-free soap and dry with a clean towel.

Step 2: Rinse with Lukewarm Water

Run your glasses under a gentle stream of lukewarm (not hot) water. This removes dust and debris that would otherwise act like sandpaper when you wipe. Hot water can damage lens coatings.

Step 3: Apply a Drop of Lotion-Free Dish Soap

One tiny drop per lens. Gently rub with your fingertips — not your nails — across both sides of both lenses, the nose pads, and the frame. Avoid soaps with moisturizers, which leave a film.

Step 4: Rinse Thoroughly

Make sure all soap is gone. Residual soap causes smearing.

Step 5: Dry with a Clean Microfiber Cloth

Use the microfiber cloth that came with your glasses, or buy a multi-pack. Never use: paper towels (wood fibers scratch lenses), your shirt (trapped dust and fabric softener residue), or toilet paper.

What NOT to Use

  • Windex or glass cleaner: Contains ammonia that strips anti-reflective coating
  • Vinegar: Too acidic for lens coatings
  • Isopropyl alcohol (on coated lenses): Can damage anti-reflective and anti-scratch coatings over time
  • Saliva: Contains oils and bacteria that smear lenses and can damage coatings
  • Dry microfiber cloth on dirty lenses: Always rinse first to remove abrasive particles

How Often Should You Clean Your Glasses?

Daily: Quick rinse and dry with microfiber cloth.
Weekly: Full wash with dish soap.
Monthly: Ultrasonic cleaning (most optical shops offer this for free) to deep-clean nose pads and hinges.

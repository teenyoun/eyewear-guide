Best Clip-On Sunglasses for Prescription Glasses — No Compromise Sun Protection

August 08, 2026 · 6 min read

Prescription sunglasses cost $100-300 and require carrying a second pair everywhere. Clip-on sunglasses solve this for under $30 — they attach to your existing glasses when the sun comes out and flip up or detach when you go inside. Here are the best options for every frame type and budget.

1. Eyekepper Polarized Clip-Ons — Best Universal Value

Eyekepper's clip-ons are the best-selling option on Amazon for good reason. Under $15, you get polarized lenses with UV400 protection in a universal flip-up design. The spring-loaded clip attaches to the bridge of most glasses — it works with metal and plastic frames, though the fit is more secure on frames with a defined bridge. Available in multiple sizes and lens shapes to match your frame style.

2. PolarSpex Magnetic Clip-Ons — Best Premium Magnetic

PolarSpex uses small adhesive-backed magnets that attach to your existing frames (one-time installation) and a matching polarized lens that clicks on magnetically. The result looks like a single pair of prescription sunglasses — no visible clips. The adhesive holds for months but can be removed without residue. Best for metal frames; the magnets don't adhere as well to textured plastic.

3. Chemistrie Custom Clip-Ons — Best Custom Fit

Chemistrie makes clip-ons custom-measured to your exact frame model. They use microscopic magnets embedded in the lenses (installed by an optician) that are invisible when the clip-on is removed. The fit is perfect because it's made for your specific frame. At $60-90, they are the most expensive option but completely invisible — no one will know they aren't prescription sunglasses.

The Anti-Reflective Coating Trick

Clip-ons work best with prescription glasses that have anti-reflective (AR) coating on the back surface of the lenses. Without AR coating, light reflects between your glasses and the clip-on, creating ghost images. If you plan to use clip-ons regularly, make sure your prescription glasses include AR coating — it's a $5-10 upgrade that makes a dramatic difference.

How to Read Your Glasses Prescription — SPH, CYL, AXIS Explained

August 18, 2026 · 6 min read

Your glasses prescription looks like a string of cryptic numbers and abbreviations — but every one of them controls something your lenses do. Understanding your prescription helps you order glasses online with confidence and catch errors in what the lab fills. Here is what every field means.

The Basics

Reading Your SPH Number

SPH (sphere) is the main power. A minus sign (-) means myopia — you see clearly near, blurry far. A plus sign (+) means hyperopia — you see clearly far, but near vision requires effort. The number is measured in diopters (D): each 0.25 is a step, so -2.50 is stronger than -2.00. Prescriptions above ±6.00 are considered high.

Understanding CYL and AXIS

Astigmatism happens when your cornea is shaped more like a football than a basketball. CYL is the extra lens power needed to fix it, and AXIS is the angle where that correction applies — from 1 to 180 degrees. A prescription without CYL means no astigmatism correction. Higher CYL (above -1.50) requires more precise lens alignment; slight axis errors cause tilting images.

What ADD Means

ADD appears on prescriptions for people over 40 — it's the extra plus power added at the bottom for reading. Single-vision prescriptions have no ADD. If you see ADD +2.00, your lenses need multifocal design (bifocal or progressive).

Common Prescription Patterns

  • Simple myopia: OD -2.00 / OS -2.25 — single-vision, easy to fill online
  • Myopia + astigmatism: OD -3.00 -0.75 x 175 — needs precise axis alignment
  • Presbyopia (40+): OD -1.00 ADD +2.00 — multifocal needed
  • Prism: Rare, written as prism values — requires professional lab handling

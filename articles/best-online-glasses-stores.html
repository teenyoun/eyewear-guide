What You Need to Order Prescription Glasses Online

Before you start shopping, make sure you have:

  1. Your prescription (Rx): Including sphere (SPH), cylinder (CYL), axis, and add power (for bifocals/progressives). Your optometrist is legally required to give you a copy.
  2. Pupillary Distance (PD): The distance between your pupils in millimeters. Your prescription may or may not include this. Zenni and others have free online tools to measure it with a webcam or credit card for scale.
  3. Your frame measurements: If you have a current pair that fits well, measure the frame width, bridge width, and temple length (usually printed on the inside of the arm).

Lens Upgrade Guide

Most online stores offer lens upgrades. Here's when each is worth it:

  • Anti-reflective (AR) coating: Always worth it. Reduces glare, improves night driving, makes lenses nearly invisible in photos. Usually $5-15.
  • Blue light filtering: Worth it if you use screens late at night. See our evidence review. Usually $15-30.
  • High-index lenses: Worth it if your prescription is stronger than ±3.00. Makes lenses thinner and lighter. Cost increases with index (1.61 → 1.67 → 1.74).
  • Photochromic (Transitions): Worth it if you want one pair for indoor and outdoor use. Turns dark in sunlight. Usually $40-80.
  • Progressive (no-line bifocals): Only if you need them. Online progressives have improved dramatically but still have a higher remake rate than single-vision.
Pro tip: Order one cheap pair from Zenni or Payne Glasses first to verify your prescription and PD are correct. Once you're confident, order more expensive pairs from Warby Parker or EyeBuyDirect during a sale.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are online glasses as good as store-bought?

For single-vision prescriptions, yes — often better value. For complex prescriptions (progressives, prism correction, very high astigmatism), in-store fitting gives more reliable results, though online stores are catching up.

What if the glasses don't fit or I don't like them?

All the stores we listed have return policies. Read them carefully — some offer full refunds (Warby Parker, Liingo), while others are more restrictive (Zenni offers 50% back). Always try them on immediately after delivery to stay within the return window.

Can I use my vision insurance?

Some online stores accept insurance directly; most let you submit a claim for reimbursement. Check your plan — many include an out-of-network reimbursement option for online purchases.

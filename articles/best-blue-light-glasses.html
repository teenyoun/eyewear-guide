  • Build quality: Hinge durability, frame flexibility, coating resistance to scratches

What to Look For in Blue Light Glasses

When shopping, focus on these four factors:

  1. Lens clarity: Look for "low color distortion" or "high transmittance" in product descriptions. Cheaper lenses give everything a yellow cast.
  2. Frame fit: Check frame width measurements against your face. Most Amazon listings include a size guide image.
  3. Anti-reflective coating: Essential for reducing glare from overhead lights and windows.
  4. Blue light blocking percentage: Aim for 40-60% in the 400-450nm range for daytime use. Higher percentages (80%+) are better for evening/night use but will have more color tint.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can blue light glasses be worn all day?

Yes. All the glasses we tested are designed for all-day wear. If you choose a pair with heavy tint (80%+ blocking), you may find colors look slightly warmer, which could be distracting for design work. For all-day office use, we recommend 40-60% blocking with low color distortion.

Do I need blue light glasses if my screen has a night mode?

Night mode (like Apple's Night Shift or Windows Night Light) reduces blue light at the software level, which helps but isn't as effective as physical filtering. Using both together provides the best protection, especially in the evening.

Can I wear blue light glasses over my prescription glasses?

It depends on the frame size. Some of the larger frames like Gaoye can fit over smaller prescription glasses, but it's not ideal. Consider prescription blue light glasses from online retailers like Zenni or Warby Parker instead — see our online glasses stores guide.

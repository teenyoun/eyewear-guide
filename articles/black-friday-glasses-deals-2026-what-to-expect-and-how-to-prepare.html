Black Friday Glasses Deals 2026 — What to Expect and How to Prepare

September 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Black Friday 2026 falls on Friday, November 27 — the day after Thanksgiving (November 26). But the useful part of the weekend starts earlier than the Friday: most eyewear promotions open during Thanksgiving week, and a fair number launch one to two weeks before. If you wait until Friday morning to start looking, you are comparing discounts without knowing what any of it cost three weeks ago.

This is a preparation guide, not a rumour roundup. It covers which eyewear categories genuinely reach their yearly low, which ones get a "discount" that means nothing, and the five things worth having ready before the sales open.

Why Sunglasses Are the Real Opportunity

Sunwear is the one eyewear category where holiday discounts are structural rather than theatrical. Sunglasses are seasonal stock: a retailer holding November inventory of a summer product has a real reason to clear it, and the item will be replaced by a new model line in spring anyway. That is a genuine incentive to discount, which is why this is where the yearly low usually lands.

Two specifications carry the whole decision, and both are printed on the listing:

  • UV400 or "100% UV protection". Non-negotiable. A dark tint without UV400 is worse than no sunglasses at all, because your pupil dilates behind the tint and takes in more ultraviolet light. If the listing does not state a UV rating, assume there is none.
  • Polarization. A separate feature from UV. It removes horizontal glare bouncing off wet roads, water and snow. Worth paying for if you drive or spend time near water; not required for walking around a city.

Why the "Free Frame" Prescription Deal Is Usually the Worst Offer

Prescription bundles look like the headline deal of the weekend, and they are the one to read most carefully. The reason is where the money actually sits in a pair of glasses: the frame is the cheap component and the lens is the expensive one.

So when a promotion discounts the frame to zero, it discounts the part that was never costing much. The lens — the part that determines whether you can see, whether the coating survives a year of cleaning, and whether the optics are centred over your pupils — is usually specified as vaguely as possible: "standard single vision", no index, no coating named, no mention of who manufactured it. What that typically means in practice is the thickest lens material in the catalogue with the least durable coating.

None of this makes the bundle a scam. It makes it a bundle with a specification you have to ask about. Three questions settle it:

  1. What lens index is included? Anything above about −2.00 should not be 1.50 index unless you want visible edge thickness.
  2. Is anti-reflective coating included, and is it warranted? A named scratch warranty is the difference between a coating and a coating that survives.
  3. Whose lens is it? A named lens manufacturer is a real signal; silence is an answer too.

If the answers are vague, the bundle is not a deal — it is a cheap frame with an unknown lens, and you will replace it within the year. Our full cost breakdown shows where the money goes in each price band.

How to Prepare (Do This Before the Weekend)

  1. Get your prescription and your PD in writing. You are entitled to your prescription after an exam, and ordering online requires your pupillary distance — the number people most often forget to ask for. Here is how to measure PD at home, and what SPH, CYL and AXIS mean once you have the paper.
  2. Write your list by category, not by product. "Sunglasses with UV400", "spare readers", "hard case", "lens cloths". Categories survive hype; specific products get substituted when stock runs out.
  3. Record today's prices. A discount only means something against a baseline. Note the current price and the list price on two or three candidates now — a week later you will be looking at a "was" figure that nobody ever charged.
  4. Decide your ceiling before you shop. Sale weekends are designed to move the number you had in mind. A written maximum per item is the cheapest tool available.
  5. Check the return window, not the discount. For anything worn against your face, the return policy matters more than the percentage.

FAQ

When do the glasses sales actually start?

Most open during Thanksgiving week (the week of November 23 in 2026), with some launching one to two weeks earlier and a few holding their best price for the Friday itself. Practically: start watching prices about two weeks out, and treat Friday as the deadline rather than the start.

Is Black Friday better than the fall Prime event for glasses?

They discount different things. The fall Prime event is stronger on accessories and consumables, while Black Friday is where seasonal sunwear inventory is genuinely cleared. If you want one pair of good sunglasses, Black Friday is the better bet; if you want cases, cloths and spare readers, either works.

Should I buy prescription glasses in a Black Friday bundle?

Only if the lens specification is named and you are satisfied with it. Ask about lens index, coating and manufacturer before you order — a free frame with an unnamed lens is the least valuable version of the offer.

How do I avoid a fake discount?

Compare the current price against the price graph on that listing and against other sellers of the same item. A high "was" price that was never charged is the most common trick in every holiday sale.

What is actually worth buying for someone else?

Anything that does not need a prescription: a hard case, a good lens cloth, a cleaning kit, or a spare pair of readers. A decent case is the safest useful gift in the category — it cannot be the wrong strength or the wrong fit.

The Bottom Line

Prepare in the last week of November by writing down your prescription, your PD and today's prices on two or three candidates. Then buy sunwear and accessories, skip the free-frame bundles unless the lens is specified, and check the price history on everything else. That is the entire strategy — the discount is only real relative to a number you wrote down first.

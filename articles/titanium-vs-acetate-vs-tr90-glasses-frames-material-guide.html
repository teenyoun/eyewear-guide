Titanium vs Acetate vs TR90 Glasses Frames — Material Guide

July 31, 2026 · 6 min read

The frame material matters as much as the lenses. The wrong material means uncomfortable glasses that break or lose shape. Here is how to choose between titanium, acetate, and TR90 — the three most common frame materials in 2026.

Titanium — Best for All-Day Wear

Pure titanium or beta-titanium frames are the gold standard for comfort. They are so light you forget you are wearing them. The flexibility is remarkable — bend the temples nearly flat and they spring back. Hypoallergenic, so ideal for sensitive skin. The main drawback: titanium frames are more expensive and have a limited style range — mostly thin metal designs in silver, gold, or matte black.

Acetate — Best for Style

Acetate is a plant-based plastic derived from cotton and wood pulp. Unlike cheap injection-molded plastic, acetate is cut from solid blocks, giving it a rich depth of color and pattern (tortoiseshell, marble, transparent gradients). High-end brands like Warby Parker and Ray-Ban use acetate for their best frames. The trade-off: acetate is heavier and can snap if bent too far — it is rigid, not flexible.

TR90 — Best Budget/Sports

TR90 is a thermoplastic material that is nearly indestructible. You can twist, bend, and sit on TR90 frames without breaking them. They weigh about half as much as acetate and cost a fraction of titanium. The ideal choice for kids glasses, sports glasses, or anyone who is hard on their eyewear. The only real downside: TR90 has a slightly plasticky feel and limited color depth compared to acetate.

Which Should You Choose?

  • Office worker / professional: Titanium — all-day comfort, looks polished
  • Fashion-conscious: Acetate — bold colors, premium look, luxury feel
  • Active / clumsy / kids: TR90 — nearly unbreakable, affordable to replace
  • Best value hybrid: Titanium front + TR90 temples — many online retailers offer this combination

Anti-Reflective Coating vs Blue Light Filter — Worth Paying for Both?

August 14, 2026 · 6 min read

When ordering glasses online, you will see two popular lens add-ons: anti-reflective (AR) coating and blue light filtering. They are often bundled together under names like 'digital protection' or 'screen shield' — but they do completely different things. Here is what each one actually does, what it costs, and whether you need both.

What Each One Does

Anti-Reflective (AR) Coating

AR coating is a microscopically thin multilayer film applied to lens surfaces. It reduces surface reflections from ~8% (uncoated plastic lens) to less than 1%. The result:

  • Lenses appear nearly invisible — better appearance in photos and face-to-face
  • Less glare from overhead lights, windows, and headlights when driving at night
  • More light reaches your eye — slightly better vision in low light

AR coating works on all reflected light, regardless of color or source. It is not specific to blue light — it cuts glare across the entire visible spectrum.

Blue Light Filtering

Blue light filtering specifically targets high-energy visible (HEV) light in the 380-500nm range — the blue-violet end of the spectrum. It works either through:

  • Absorptive pigment: The lens material itself contains a blue-absorbing compound. Adds a slight yellow/amber tint.
  • Reflective coating: A specialized coating that reflects blue wavelengths. Creates a blue or purple reflection on the lens surface (visible in photos).

The purpose: reduce blue light exposure in the hours before bedtime, when blue light suppresses melatonin production. See our full evidence review at Do Blue Light Glasses Really Work?

The Cost Analysis

AR coating: $5-15 online, $50-100 in-store. Almost always worth it — the cheapest upgrade with the biggest quality-of-life improvement.

Blue light filter: $15-30 online, $50-100 in-store. Worth it if you use screens within 2 hours of bedtime. Not worth it if your main screen time is during daylight hours.

Both combined: Many online retailers offer a 'digital protection' package that bundles AR + blue light filtering for $20-40 total. This is the best value if you want both.

The One Case to Avoid Blue Light

If you drive at night frequently, do not get blue light filtering on your primary glasses. Any lens that blocks blue light also reduces overall light transmission, decreasing contrast sensitivity — exactly what you need for night driving. Keep a separate pair of blue light glasses for home screen use, and use AR-coated-only glasses for driving.

  2. Blue light filtering on your home/evening glasses: Not on your driving glasses.

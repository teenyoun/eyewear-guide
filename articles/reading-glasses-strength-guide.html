Reading Glasses Strength Guide — How to Find Your Power

Updated July 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Buying reading glasses without a prescription is like buying shoes without knowing your size — you'll probably get it wrong on the first try. The good news: finding your reading glasses strength is surprisingly easy, and you can do it at home in under 5 minutes.

The Quick Method: Age-Based Chart

Presbyopia (age-related near-vision loss) affects everyone at a fairly predictable rate. While individual variation exists, this chart gives you a reliable starting point:

Age RangeRecommended Starting StrengthNotes
40-44+1.00May only need glasses for very small print
45-49+1.25 to +1.50Most common first-time reader strength
50-54+1.50 to +1.75Reading small text becomes consistently difficult
55-59+2.00 to +2.25Most people at this age use readers full-time for near work
60-64+2.25 to +2.50Near vision focus range continues to decrease
65++2.50 to +3.00Consider progressive lenses for multiple distances

The DIY Test: Printable Eye Chart Method

For a more accurate reading, use a printable diopter chart. Here's how:

  1. Print a diopter test chart at actual size (100% scale, not fit-to-page).
  2. Hold the chart exactly 14 inches from your face in good lighting.
  3. Without wearing any glasses, read the smallest line you can see clearly.
  4. The diopter number next to that line is your approximate strength.
  5. If between two strengths, choose the lower number — too much magnification causes eye strain.

The Trial Method: In-Store Test

The most reliable DIY approach is trying different strengths in person:

  1. Visit any drugstore, pharmacy, or big-box store with a reading glasses rack.
  2. Start with +1.00 and read something at your typical reading distance (usually 14-16 inches for books, 20-24 inches for computer screens).
  3. If text is blurry, go up in +0.25 increments until it's sharp and comfortable.
  4. If text is sharp at +1.50 and +1.75, choose the weaker one (+1.50).
  5. Once you know your strength, you can order better-quality readers online at the same power.

Computer Reading Glasses: A Special Case

If you use readers primarily for computer work (not books or phones), you need a weaker strength. A computer screen is typically 20-26 inches away — about 1.5x farther than reading distance. Subtract 0.50 to 0.75 from your reading strength:

  • If your reading strength is +2.00, your computer strength is roughly +1.25 to +1.50
  • Some brands sell "computer readers" or "mid-range readers" specifically for this distance
  • For all-day screen use, consider blue light glasses with magnification

When to See an Eye Doctor Instead

OTC reading glasses work for most people with presbyopia, but see an optometrist if:

  • Your eyes need different strengths (e.g., +1.50 in one eye, +2.00 in the other)
  • You have astigmatism (blurry vision at all distances)
  • You need correction for both distance and near (consider progressives)
  • You experience eye pain, frequent headaches, or sudden vision changes
  • You haven't had an eye exam in over 2 years

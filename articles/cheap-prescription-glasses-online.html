How Online Glasses Stores Keep Prices Low

Online retailers cut costs in three ways:

  1. Vertical integration: Companies like Zenni own their manufacturing facilities. No wholesale markup.
  2. No retail overhead: No rent for prime shopping locations, no sales staff commissions, no inventory display costs.
  3. Direct-to-consumer model: Frames and lenses go from factory to your door, cutting out distributors and optical shops.

The result: frames that cost $5-15 to produce are sold for $7-30 online, vs. $150-400 in stores. Lenses follow the same pattern.

How to Minimize Your Total Cost

  1. Skip unnecessary upgrades: You need AR coating. You probably don't need blue light, photochromic, or high-index lenses unless your prescription is strong (±3.00 or higher).
  2. Wait for sales: EyeBuyDirect and GlassesUSA run BOGO/40-50% off sales almost monthly. Set a price alert.
  3. Use new customer deals: Firmoo's first-pair-free, Zenni's $5 off first order — stack these whenever possible.
  4. Order multiple pairs at once: Many stores offer free shipping at $50-60+, so bundling 2-3 pairs saves $5-10 on shipping.
  5. Buy frames only from Amazon, lenses elsewhere: If you find frames you love on Amazon for $10-15, you can send them to services like Lensabl for prescription lens installation (~$77 for basic single-vision). Total cost: ~$90 — still far less than $300 in-store.
Pro tip: Your pupillary distance (PD) isn't always on your prescription. Before ordering, measure it using Zenni's free online PD tool or ask your optometrist during your next exam. Getting PD wrong is the #1 reason for returns.

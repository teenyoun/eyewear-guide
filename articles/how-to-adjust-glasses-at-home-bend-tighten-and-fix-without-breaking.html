How to Adjust Glasses at Home — Bend, Tighten, and Fix Without Breaking

August 13, 2026 · 6 min read

Glasses inevitably go out of alignment. One temple arm bends out, the nose pads spread, the frame tilts, and suddenly your vision feels wrong and your glasses leave marks on your nose. Most adjustments take 5 minutes with zero tools — here is how to do each one safely.

Warning: These methods work for most standard frames. Do NOT attempt on titanium memory metal frames (they require special heating equipment), frames with embedded electronics (audio glasses), or extremely thin/expensive designer frames — those go to a professional.

Problem 1: Glasses Sit Crooked on Your Face

Diagnosis: Place glasses on a flat table. If one temple arm is higher than the other, the frame is twisted.

Fix for plastic/acetate frames: Hold the frame by the lenses (not the bridge). Use a hairdryer on medium heat for 20-30 seconds on the bridge area to soften the plastic. Gently twist the frame in the direction that levels the arms — or hold the higher arm down on the table and gently press the lower side of the frame bridge down to twist it back. Let cool naturally. Check on table — repeat until arms are even.

Fix for metal frames: No heat needed. Hold the frame with both hands — one on each lens. Gently twist the bridge in the opposite direction of the tilt. Metal frames are springy and forgiving — small adjustments add up.

Problem 2: Glasses Slide Down

Fix for plastic/acetate: Heat the temple bend (the curved part that hooks behind your ear) with a hairdryer for 20 seconds. Bend the curve slightly tighter — the goal is more hook behind the ear. Start subtle — you can always bend more, but over-bending a plastic frame can snap it.

Fix for metal: Use needlenose pliers (wrap tips in electrical tape to prevent scratching). Gently increase the curve of the temple bend behind your ear. Metal temples are designed to be adjusted — this is the intended way to fix fit.

Fix for nose-fit (metal nose pads): Pinch the nose pad arms closer together. This raises the frame higher on your nose and increases grip. Spread them apart to lower the frame.

Problem 3: Glasses Pinch Your Temples (Too Tight)

Fix for plastic/acetate: Heat the temple arms (from the hinge to the ear bend) with a hairdryer for 30 seconds. Hold the frame by the bridge and gently spread the temple arms outward — a few degrees at a time. Let cool and try on. Repeat until pressure is comfortable but glasses stay in place.

Fix for metal: Grip the temple near the hinge with needlenose pliers and bend outward slightly. Less force needed than for plastic.

Problem 4: One Lens Sits Closer to Your Eye

This causes one eye to see a slightly different image size — a recipe for eye strain.

Fix for metal frames: Adjust the nose pad on the side where the lens is too close — spread that one nose pad outward (away from the lens). Or adjust the pad on the too-far side inward. View the glasses from above — both lenses should be the same distance from an imaginary plane across your face.

Fix for plastic frames: More difficult — the bridge may need heating and gentle reshaping. If this is a persistent issue with plastic frames, see an optician.

Problem 5: Screws Are Loose

A loose hinge screw causes the temple arm to wobble and eventually fall off. Get a precision screwdriver set (under $8 on Amazon) and tighten the screw. Apply a tiny drop of clear nail polish or Loctite (blue, not red) to the screw threads before tightening — this prevents it from loosening again. Check all screws monthly.

Essential Tools to Own

  • Precision screwdriver set with flathead and Phillips (#00, #0, #1)
  • Needlenose pliers with padded tips (or wrap regular pliers in electrical tape)
  • Hairdryer (for plastic frame adjustments)
  • Replacement screws and nose pads (available in multi-packs under $5)
  • Microfiber cloth (always clean before adjusting — dirt scratches frames when you grip them)

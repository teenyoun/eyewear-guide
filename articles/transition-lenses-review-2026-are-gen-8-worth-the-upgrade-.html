Transitions Optical released their 8th generation photochromic technology in early 2026, promising 30% faster fade-back (clearing indoors) and darker outdoor performance. We compared Gen 8 against the previous Gen 7 and budget generic photochromic lenses to see if the premium is justified.

Key Takeaways

Gen 8 is noticeably faster — especially the fade-back time. Walking indoors goes from a 2-minute awkward squint (Gen 7) to about 90 seconds (Gen 8). The difference matters if you frequently move between indoors and outdoors.

Generic photochromic is not terrible — at $20-40, it is perfectly functional. The main compromise is a slight yellow tint when clear and slower transition speeds. For budget-conscious buyers who do not need rapid transitions, generic is fine.

Who Should Get Gen 8?

  • People who move in/out frequently (retail workers, delivery drivers, dog walkers)
  • Those sensitive to the purple-gray residual tint of older generations
  • Anyone willing to pay for the best available photochromic technology

Who Should Skip It?

  • Budget buyers — Gen 7 is still very good and getting discounted now that Gen 8 is out
  • People who mostly stay indoors or mostly stay outdoors — just get two separate pairs instead
  • Drivers — photochromic lenses still do not darken behind UV-blocking car windshields
